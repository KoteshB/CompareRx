MedsCheck

A medicine interaction and risk checker

Plan and architecture overview
The problem

When prescriptions are on paper, nobody checks the whole list

Mrs. Rao, 72, sees a cardiologist and an orthopaedic doctor. Both prescribe a painkiller, under different brand names. Neither knows about the other.

The two brands share an active ingredient. She takes both.

  • Medicines prescribed on paper (not in an electronic record) get no automatic check.
  • Each doctor prescribes without seeing what others prescribed.
  • The result can be double dosing or an unsafe combination.
  • Patients, often elderly, rarely know what to ask.
The product

A patient enters a list. Every answer comes with its source.

Principles

Four promises shape every design choice

No source, no answer

Every statement carries a source and an exact quote. With no source, the tool says “I am unable to find the answer” instead of guessing.

No data stored

Medicine lists, and later images, are never saved or logged. Saving per user is a separate, opt-in phase after a privacy review.

Always refer to a professional

A fixed notice appears on every result. The tool never says medicines are “safe” and never advises changing a dose.

Read, don’t guess

In the photo versions, only text visible in the image can become a medicine name, and the patient confirms every one.

Trust

Software, not the AI, decides what the patient sees

A missing finding is acceptable. An invented one is not. A real quote can still fail to support a claim, so clinicians review the test set as well.

Architecture

The proof of concept is a small, stateless backend

Roadmap

Prove the sourcing first, then add photos, then polish

Saving medicines per user changes the “nothing is stored” promise, so it waits for its own privacy and legal review.

Versions 2 and 3

Patients can add medicines from a photo, and always confirm what was read

Version 2: medicine strip

Photograph a strip or box. An AI agent checks the image quality, reads the name and strength, matches it to the drug data, and shows the result next to the photo.

Version 3: handwritten prescription

Upload a prescription. The agent separates medicine lines from doctor and clinic details, shows how sure it is for each line, and offers candidates for unclear words.

  • Read, don’t guess. Unclear text is reported as unreadable, never filled in from the AI’s own knowledge.
  • The patient confirms. Nothing is evaluated straight from a photo.
  • No images stored. Photos are processed in memory and discarded.
Quality gates

Each stage has a bar it must clear before the next begins

PoC
  • Zero unsourced claims across at least 50 test queries
  • 100% of unanswerable queries return “unable to find”
  • A planted fake quote is always removed
Version 1
  • At least 95% recall on 200 clinician-reviewed medicine lists
  • Privacy audit passed: nothing stored, nothing logged
  • Clinical and legal sign-off
Version 2
  • At least 95% of confirmed names match the strip
  • Unreadable photos are never guessed (100%)
Version 3
  • Under 1% of lines marked confident are wrong
  • Pharmacist or clinician sign-off

Targets are drafts, to be set by the clinical reviewer.

Risks and decisions

What could go wrong, and what we need decided

Main risks

  • A missed interaction could give false reassurance, so we never say “safe”.
  • Indian brand names are inconsistent, so matching may fail. Failures show as “unable to find”.
  • Health guidance carries legal exposure.
  • The AI provider may retain request data.

Decisions needed

  • Which approved drug databases to use, and their licences
  • Who the clinical reviewer is
  • Legal review of health-data rules, including saved lists
  • AI provider’s data retention terms for our account
  • Language, framework and hosting
Next steps

What happens next

  1. Choose the drug data source and confirm its licence.
  2. Name the clinical reviewer.
  3. Confirm the AI provider’s retention terms.
  4. Pick the technology stack.
  5. Build the proof-of-concept test set first: unanswerable medicines, planted fake quotes and a privacy audit.
